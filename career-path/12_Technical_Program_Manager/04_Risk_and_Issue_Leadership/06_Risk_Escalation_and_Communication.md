# Risk Escalation and Communication

> **Core skill:** Escalating risks before they become issues — with options, not alarms — and communicating risk posture to stakeholders in the language of their concerns (dates, costs, trade-offs), not the language of the risk register.

## Why This Matters

Risk communication fails in two directions. The first is silence: risks exist in the register but never reach the people who can authorize the mitigation, allocate the contingency, or make the trade-off. The risk fires, and the sponsor's first question is "why didn't anyone tell me?" — which is the most expensive question in program management.

The second is noise: every amber flicker in the register is escalated with alarm, the sponsor's inbox fills with risk alerts, and the signal-to-noise ratio collapses. The sponsor stops reading, and the genuinely critical risk that needed a decision is buried among a dozen false alarms.

The TPM's escalation discipline navigates between silence and noise. Risks are escalated at the right threshold, to the right person, with the right package — so that escalations are received as competence, not panic, and result in decisions within one governance cycle. Risk communication is not "here is what we are worried about." It is "here is the decision you need to make, with the information you need to make it, by the date it must be made."

## The Escalation Threshold

Not every risk belongs in the sponsor's inbox. The TPM defines escalation thresholds at program setup:

| Escalate When | Do Not Escalate When |
|---------------|----------------------|
| The mitigation requires authority above the program's level | The mitigation is within the program's authority and resources |
| The risk, if it fires, will affect a commitment the sponsor owns | The impact is contained within the program's contingency |
| A decision is needed that the program team cannot make (scope, budget, date trade-off) | The decision is within the program's delegated authority |
| The aggregate risk exposure exceeds the program's contingency | Contingency covers the exposure |
| A risk has been at critical status for two reporting cycles without resolution | The risk has an active mitigation and is trending toward retirement |

The threshold is defined in the program's governance charter and agreed with the sponsor at program start. It is a contract: the TPM will not bother the sponsor with risks below this line, and the sponsor will act when a risk crosses it.

## The Escalation Package

Escalation is a structured communication, not a status update:

| Element | Content | Purpose |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Headline** | One sentence: the risk, the decision needed, the deadline | Grab attention; frame the decision |
| **Situation** | What has changed: risk score, trigger proximity, mitigation status | Context without requiring the recipient to reconstruct it |
| **Impact** | What happens if the risk fires or the decision is not made: quantified effect on schedule, scope, cost, quality | Make the cost of inaction visible |
| **Options** | Two or three resolution paths with pros, cons, feasibility, and cost | Enable choice; demonstrate that analysis has been done |
| **Recommendation** | The TPM's recommended option with rationale | Reduce decision burden; demonstrate judgment |
| **Decision needed by** | The date by which a decision must be made, and why that date matters (what expires after it) | Create urgency tied to real constraints |

The package is delivered in writing before any meeting. The meeting is for discussion and decision, not for the recipient to learn the situation for the first time.

## Escalation Levels

| Level | Who | Escalate When | Format |
|-------|-----|---------------|--------|
| **Program team** | Workstream leads, TPM | Risk crosses critical threshold; mitigation off-track | RAID log review; flagged in program status |
| **Program sponsor** | Executive sponsor | Risk affects sponsor-owned commitment; mitigation exceeds program authority; contingency exhausted | Escalation package; sponsor meeting |
| **Steering committee** | Governance body above sponsor | Cross-program trade-off; strategic decision; program date or budget change | Steering committee package; pre-briefed with sponsor |

The TPM escalates one level at a time. Skipping the sponsor to go directly to the steering committee is a political error — the sponsor is either the TPM's ally in the escalation or a blocker who was not given the chance to act first.

## Risk Communication to Different Audiences

| Audience | What They Care About | How to Communicate Risk |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Sponsor** | Date, budget, reputation — will this program deliver what I promised? | Risk to commitments; decisions needed; contingency position |
| **Engineering teams** | What does this mean for our work? Do we need to change anything? | Impact on their deliverables; dependency changes; mitigation actions they need to take |
| **Stakeholders (dependent teams)** | Will this affect the thing I am depending on? | Impact on their dependency; revised timeline if applicable |
| **External partners** | Are we meeting our commitments? Are you meeting yours? | Their risk status and mitigation; program's risk to them |
| **Program team** | What is the full picture? What needs attention? | Complete risk register; trends; systemic issues |

The TPM translates risk into each audience's language. The engineering team does not need a probability-impact matrix. The sponsor does not need the technical root cause. Both need the information they can act on, in the format they can consume.

## The Risk Narrative

Beyond individual risk escalations, the TPM maintains a risk narrative: a coherent story of the program's risk posture that connects individual risks into a picture stakeholders can understand.

| Narrative Element | Content |
|-------------------|---------|
| **Current posture** | Aggregate risk exposure; top risks; trajectory (improving, stable, deteriorating) |
| **What has changed** | Risks retired, new risks identified, risks that crossed thresholds |
| **What is being done** | Active mitigations; contingency status; escalation decisions in flight |
| **What is needed** | Decisions, resources, authority — the TPM's ask |
| **Forward look** | Risks on the horizon; what the next period's risk posture looks like |

The narrative is updated for every sponsor review and steering committee meeting. It is the answer to "how risky is this program right now?" — answered honestly, with evidence, in under five minutes.

## Communicating Risk Honesty

| Situation | Weak Communication | Honest Communication |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Program is at high risk but on track this period | "Everything is green" | "We are on track this period, but the risk posture has deteriorated. Here is what changed and what we are doing." |
| Mitigation is not working | "Mitigation is in progress" | "The current mitigation is not reducing exposure as expected. We are evaluating alternative strategies and will recommend one by [date]." |
| Program cannot absorb another slip | "We have contingency" | "Our contingency covers our current exposure with no margin. Any additional slip will require a date or scope decision." |
| A risk is likely to fire | "We are watching it" | "This risk is assessed at 70% probability. We have a contingency plan and recommend the sponsor review it now, while options are still viable." |

Risk honesty is the TPM's core credibility. Stakeholders forgive bad news delivered early with options. They do not forgive bad news delivered late with excuses. The TPM's rule: the sponsor should never learn about a program threat from someone other than the TPM.

## Practical Applications

- [ ] Escalation thresholds are defined in the governance charter and agreed with the sponsor
- [ ] Every escalation carries: headline, situation, impact, options, recommendation, decision deadline
- [ ] Risk communication is translated for each audience: sponsor, teams, stakeholders, partners
- [ ] A risk narrative is maintained and updated for every sponsor review
- [ ] Risk honesty is the TPM's non-negotiable standard — bad news early, with options

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Escalation as alarm** | "The program is at risk!" with no analysis, options, or decision | Escalation is a decision-support package, not an alert |
| **Risk sanitization** | Risks are softened, probabilities rounded down, impacts minimized | Honest assessment; the sponsor needs the real picture |
| **One-size communication** | The same risk report goes to the sponsor, teams, and partners | Translate risk into each audience's language and concerns |
| **Escalation without pre-briefing** | Sponsor sees the escalation for the first time in a steering committee | Pre-brief the sponsor; they should never be surprised in public |
| **No narrative** | Individual risks reported without the connecting story | Maintain a risk narrative that connects the dots |

## Success Indicators

- The sponsor receives fewer than five risk escalations per quarter — and acts on every one
- Risk escalations result in decisions within one governance cycle
- Stakeholders can state the program's risk posture in their own words
- The sponsor never learns about a program risk from someone other than the TPM

## Summary

Risk escalation and communication is the TPM's stakeholder discipline: risks are escalated at defined thresholds, with structured packages (headline, situation, impact, options, recommendation, deadline), to the right level in the governance hierarchy. Risk communication is translated for each audience — the sponsor hears dates and decisions, the teams hear impact and actions — and the TPM maintains an honest risk narrative that connects individual risks into a coherent picture. The TPM's test: when a risk fires, did the sponsor already know about it, understand the options, and make the contingency decision — or is the first conversation happening after the damage is done?
