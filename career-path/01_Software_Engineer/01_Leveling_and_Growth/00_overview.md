# Leveling and Growth

> **Capability area:** Understanding how engineering levels are defined and measured, and building the habits that move a junior engineer to a reliable mid-level engineer.

## What Changes from Junior to Mid

A junior engineer completes well-specified tasks with regular guidance. A mid-level engineer takes a feature from a rough request to production with light supervision, surfaces problems before they become surprises, and needs help only on genuinely hard calls. This capability area owns the vault's baseline definition of that jump; every other path compares against it.

Judgment at this level shows in:
- Knowing when to ask for help and when to decide and note the assumption
- Turning vague requests into small, verifiable plans without over-scoping
- Making your work visible through artifacts, not status claims
- Using feedback as input to a growth plan rather than taking it as verdict

## Key Principles

- Levels describe scope and autonomy, not tenure or speed of typing
- The mid-level baseline is defined here and referenced by every other path in this vault
- Growth targets work in pairs: one technical, one collaborative
- Artifacts beat assertions: anything you claim about yourself should have a link behind it
- Feedback is data about your behavior in context, not a verdict on your worth

## Common Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Waiting to be told what to work on next | Shows junior-level autonomy | Propose the next task with a one-line rationale |
| Hiding uncertainty until review | Surprises erode trust | Surface assumptions the moment you notice them |
| Collecting titles instead of skills | Titles vary per company; skills transfer | Measure growth against the five dimensions |
| Comparing yourself to the fastest typist | Speed is the least differentiating skill | Compare judgment, scope, and reliability |
| Saving feedback for the annual review | Corrections arrive too late to matter | Ask for one specific piece of feedback every month |

## Maturity Signals

- You can state your current level's expectations and name two gaps without prompting
- Your manager confirms your self-assessment is roughly accurate
- You finish features with fewer than two rounds of scope correction
- Colleagues route questions about your area to you first
- Your brag document has dated entries from the last four weeks
