# Readiness for Senior

> **Capability area:** The practical bridge from reliable mid-level execution to senior-level ownership — what changes, what stalls people, and what evidence to build along the way.

## What Changes from Junior to Mid (and What's Next)

This module closes the entry path. The jump ahead — mid to senior — is the shift from executing agreed work to owning problems: framing before solving, deciding without being asked, and lifting the team's output, not just your own. Every topic here previews one senior capability and gives you the mid-level practice that builds it.

Judgment at this level shows in:
- Volunteering for ownership of a small system area before you feel "ready"
- Framing problems and writing the plan, not just estimating the tasks
- Making design calls on medium-risk changes and defending the trade-offs
- Building the evidence trail that the mid-to-senior case will need later

## Key Principles

- Senior readiness is built in mid-level work: the scope grows before the title does
- Ownership is taken, not granted; small system areas are the training ground
- Problem framing is the highest-leverage senior skill and the easiest to practice early
- Design judgment is a muscle built on medium-risk decisions with written trade-offs
- Evidence collected during growth beats evidence assembled during a promotion cycle

## Common Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Waiting until you "feel senior" to act senior | The feeling follows the behavior, not the reverse | Take one senior behavior per quarter now |
| Big-bang ownership grabs | Overreach erodes trust fast | Grow scope one adjacent area at a time |
| Skipping framing because the ticket exists | You optimize the wrong problem | Restate the problem and success criteria first |
| Silent design decisions | Nobody can review or learn from them | Write the decision and the trade-offs, even briefly |
| Evidence gathering only when the cycle opens | Sparse, stale, unconvincing | Keep the brag document current weekly |

## Maturity Signals

- You own at least one small system area end to end with light oversight
- Your problem statements get adopted by others before solutions are proposed
- Your design notes show options considered and reasons, not just the winner
- Your manager describes you as "already operating at senior level in most situations"
- Your evidence file would support a promotion case within a quarter or two
