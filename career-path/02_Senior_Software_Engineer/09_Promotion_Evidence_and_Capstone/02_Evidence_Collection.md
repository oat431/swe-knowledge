# Evidence Collection

## The Problem with Retroactive Documentation

Most engineers try to remember what they did when promotion time arrives. This leads to:
- **Forgotten contributions**: Significant work gets overlooked
- **Weak metrics**: Can't recall specific numbers or timelines
- **Missing artifacts**: Design docs, code reviews, and feedback are lost
- **Incomplete stories**: Can't reconstruct the full context and impact

**Solution**: Collect evidence systematically and continuously.

## The Brag Document

A brag document is a living record of your accomplishments, updated regularly (weekly or bi-weekly).

### What to Include

```markdown
# Brag Document: [Your Name]

## Week of [Date]

### Technical Contributions
- **[Project/Task]**: [What you did]
  - Impact: [Quantified result]
  - Scope: [Team/cross-team/org]
  - Artifacts: [Links to PRs, docs, etc.]

### Leadership and Influence
- **[Initiative]**: [How you led or influenced]
  - Impact: [Outcome]
  - Feedback: [Quotes or recognition]

### Mentoring and Collaboration
- **[Person/Team]**: [How you helped]
  - Impact: [Result]
  - Testimonial: [If available]

### Learning and Growth
- **[Skill/Topic]**: [What you learned]
  - Application: [How you applied it]
  - Sharing: [How you shared knowledge]

### Recognition
- **[Source]**: [Quote or summary of positive feedback]
```

### When to Update

**Weekly (15-20 minutes)**:
- Review your calendar and task list
- Note completed work and ongoing projects
- Save positive feedback (emails, Slack messages, verbal)
- Record metrics and milestones

**After Major Milestones**:
- Project launches
- Performance improvements
- Incident resolutions
- Successful migrations
- Promotions of mentees

**After Receiving Feedback**:
- Positive feedback from peers, managers, or customers
- Recognition in meetings or company-wide
- Awards or bonuses
- Thank-you messages

## Types of Evidence to Collect

### 1. Technical Artifacts

**Design Documents and RFCs**:
- Link to the document
- Your role (author, reviewer, contributor)
- Decisions you influenced
- Implementation status and impact

**Code Contributions**:
- Significant pull requests
- Code reviews you provided
- Architectural decisions in code
- Performance optimizations

**Technical Specifications**:
- API designs
- Data models
- System architecture diagrams
- Integration specifications

### 2. Metrics and Data

**Performance Metrics**:
- Before/after comparisons
- Latency improvements
- Throughput increases
- Error rate reductions
- Cost savings

**Business Metrics**:
- Revenue impact
- Customer satisfaction improvements
- Conversion rate changes
- User engagement increases

**Operational Metrics**:
- Deployment frequency
- Lead time for changes
- Mean time to recovery (MTTR)
- Change failure rate
- Uptime and availability

**Example**:
```markdown
## Performance Optimization: Search Service

**Before**:
- p99 latency: 1200ms
- Throughput: 500 requests/second
- Error rate: 2.5%

**After**:
- p99 latency: 180ms (85% improvement)
- Throughput: 2000 requests/second (4x increase)
- Error rate: 0.3% (88% reduction)

**Business Impact**:
- Enabled 40% more concurrent users
- Reduced infrastructure costs by $15K/month
- Improved customer satisfaction score from 3.2 to 4.5

**Timeline**: Q2 2024 (6 weeks)
**Scope**: Platform team, affected 3 product teams
**Artifacts**: 
- Design doc: [link]
- PR: [link]
- Metrics dashboard: [link]
```

### 3. Feedback and Recognition

**Peer Feedback**:
- Emails thanking you for help
- Slack messages with praise
- Performance review comments
- 360 feedback

**Manager Feedback**:
- 1:1 meeting notes
- Performance review highlights
- Recognition in team meetings
- Promotion recommendations

**Leadership Feedback**:
- Skip-level meeting notes
- All-hands mentions
- Executive recognition
- Strategic initiative praise

**Customer/Partner Feedback**:
- Customer testimonials
- Partner thank-you notes
- Support ticket resolutions
- User research feedback

**Example**:
```markdown
## Peer Feedback Collection

### From: Alex Chen, Senior Engineer (Platform Team)
**Date**: March 15, 2024
**Context**: After platform migration project

"Your leadership on the migration was exceptional. You not only designed a solid
architecture but also coordinated across 5 teams seamlessly. The zero-downtime
migration saved us weeks of potential issues. I learned a lot from watching you
handle the technical and organizational challenges."

**Impact Demonstrated**: Technical leadership, cross-team coordination, mentoring

### From: Maria Rodriguez, Product Manager
**Date**: April 2, 2024
**Context**: After feature launch

"Thank you for pushing back on the timeline and explaining the technical debt
we'd be creating. Your cost-benefit analysis helped us make a better decision.
The extra 2 weeks saved us months of rework later."

**Impact Demonstrated**: Engineering economics, communication, strategic influence
```

### 4. Leadership and Influence Evidence

**Technical Leadership**:
- Projects you led
- Teams you coordinated
- Decisions you drove
- Standards you established

**Cross-Team Influence**:
- Initiatives spanning multiple teams
- Architecture decisions affecting others
- Process improvements adopted widely
- Technical strategies you shaped

**Organizational Impact**:
- Company-wide contributions
- Culture and hiring contributions
- Engineering brand building
- Industry recognition (talks, blog posts)

**Example**:
```markdown
## Leadership: Observability Standards Initiative

**Context**: Each team had different logging and monitoring practices, making
cross-service debugging difficult.

**Actions**:
- Identified the problem and proposed standardization
- Created RFC for observability standards
- Presented to Engineering Leadership Team
- Coordinated adoption across 8 teams
- Created training materials and workshops
- Mentored 3 engineers to become observability champions

**Impact**:
- Standardized practices across 50+ services
- Reduced cross-service debugging time by 60%
- Established Observability Guild (still active 2 years later)
- Trained 40+ engineers on best practices

**Scope**: Organization-wide
**Timeline**: Q3-Q4 2024 (4 months)
**Artifacts**: RFC, training deck, guild charter
**Recognition**: Featured in Engineering All-Hands, mentioned in company blog
```

### 5. Mentoring and Teaching Evidence

**Direct Mentoring**:
- Engineers you've mentored
- Their career progression
- Specific skills you taught
- Feedback from mentees

**Knowledge Sharing**:
- Tech talks you've given
- Documentation you've written
- Workshops you've led
- Blog posts or articles

**Team Development**:
- Hiring contributions
- Onboarding improvements
- Code review mentorship
- Pair programming sessions

**Example**:
```markdown
## Mentoring: Junior Engineer Development

**Mentee**: Jordan Smith, Junior Engineer
**Duration**: 12 months (Jan 2024 - Jan 2025)

**Goals Set**:
1. Improve system design skills
2. Lead a medium-complexity project
3. Get promoted to mid-level engineer

**Activities**:
- Weekly 1:1 meetings (1 hour)
- Code review mentorship (3-5 PRs/week)
- Pair programming sessions (2 hours/week)
- System design practice (bi-weekly)
- Project planning guidance

**Outcomes**:
- Led authentication service refactor (medium complexity)
- Improved code review approval rate from 40% to 85%
- Promoted to mid-level in December 2024
- Now mentoring a new junior engineer

**Feedback from Jordan**:
"Your mentorship was transformative. You didn't just tell me what to do:you
helped me understand why. The system design sessions especially helped me think
about problems differently. I'm now using the same approach with my own mentee."
```

## Evidence Collection Tools

### 1. Digital Brag Document

**Options**:
- Google Doc or Notion page
- Obsidian or other note-taking app
- Spreadsheet with structured data
- Dedicated app (Bragger, Accomplishment Tracker)

**Structure**:
```markdown
# Brag Document 2024

## Q1 2024
### Week 1 (Jan 1-7)
- [Accomplishment]
- [Accomplishment]

### Week 2 (Jan 8-14)
- [Accomplishment]

[Continue for all quarters]

## Metrics Summary
| Metric | Start of Year | End of Year | Improvement |
|--------|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| [Metric 1] | [X] | [Y] | [Z%] |

## Feedback Collection
### Q1
- [Feedback]
- [Feedback]

### Q2
- [Feedback]
```

### 2. Evidence Database

**Spreadsheet columns**:
- Date
- Category (Technical, Leadership, Mentoring, etc.)
- Title
- Description
- Impact (quantified)
- Scope (Team, Cross-team, Org)
- Artifacts (links)
- Feedback (quotes)
- Related competency

**Benefits**:
- Easy to filter and sort
- Can generate reports
- Tracks metrics over time
- Identifies gaps

### 3. Calendar and Task Review

**Weekly Review Process**:
1. Review calendar for meetings, presentations, and milestones
2. Check task management system (Jira, Asana, etc.) for completed work
3. Scan email and Slack for positive feedback
4. Note any metrics or improvements
5. Update brag document

**Monthly Review Process**:
1. Summarize weekly entries into monthly highlights
2. Update metrics and progress toward goals
3. Identify patterns and themes
4. Plan evidence collection for next month

### 4. Feedback Collection System

**Proactive Feedback Requests**:
```
Hi [Name],

I'm collecting feedback for my development and would appreciate your perspective
on [specific project/initiative]. Could you share:

1. What went well from your perspective?
2. What impact did you observe?
3. Any areas where I could improve?

Thank you for your time!
```

**Feedback Tracking**:
- Create a dedicated email folder for positive feedback
- Screenshot Slack messages and save to a folder
- Keep a "kudos" document for verbal feedback
- Request written feedback after major projects

## Evidence Collection Best Practices

### 1. Be Specific and Quantified

**Weak**: "Improved system performance"
**Strong**: "Reduced p99 latency from 800ms to 120ms (85% improvement), enabling 40% more concurrent users and reducing infrastructure costs by $8K/month"

### 2. Capture Context

**Weak**: "Led platform migration"
**Strong**: "Led platform migration affecting 8 teams and 50 services, coordinating across 3 time zones, managing 15 dependencies, and completing 2 weeks ahead of schedule with zero customer-facing incidents"

### 3. Show Progression

**Weak**: Listing unrelated accomplishments
**Strong**: Showing increasing scope and responsibility over time

**Example**:
```
Q1: Owned single service (team scope)
Q2: Led service refactor (team scope)
Q3: Drove platform initiative (cross-team scope)
Q4: Established engineering standards (org scope)
```

### 4. Include Artifacts

**Always link to**:
- Design documents
- Code pull requests
- Presentations
- Metrics dashboards
- Feedback messages
- Recognition emails

### 5. Update Regularly

**Weekly**: 15-20 minutes to capture recent work
**Monthly**: 30 minutes to summarize and identify patterns
**Quarterly**: 1 hour to review progress and plan ahead

## Common Evidence Collection Mistakes

### 1. Waiting Until Promotion Time

**Problem**: Can't remember details, missing metrics, lost artifacts
**Solution**: Maintain brag document continuously

### 2. Focusing Only on Technical Work

**Problem**: Ignores leadership, mentoring, and influence
**Solution**: Track all types of contributions

### 3. Not Quantifying Impact

**Problem**: Vague descriptions don't demonstrate value
**Solution**: Always include before/after metrics and business impact

### 4. Ignoring Feedback

**Problem**: Missing peer validation of impact
**Solution**: Actively collect and save positive feedback

### 5. Not Showing Scope

**Problem**: Only documenting team-level work
**Solution**: Highlight cross-team and organizational influence

## Evidence Collection Checklist

### Weekly Tasks
- [ ] Review calendar and note accomplishments
- [ ] Check task system for completed work
- [ ] Scan email/Slack for feedback
- [ ] Update metrics and milestones
- [ ] Save artifacts and links

### Monthly Tasks
- [ ] Summarize weekly entries
- [ ] Identify patterns and themes
- [ ] Update long-term metrics
- [ ] Request feedback from 2-3 people
- [ ] Plan evidence collection for next month

### Quarterly Tasks
- [ ] Review progress toward promotion goals
- [ ] Identify evidence gaps
- [ ] Plan projects to fill gaps
- [ ] Update brag document summary
- [ ] Share highlights with manager

## Summary

Evidence collection is the foundation of a strong promotion packet. It requires:

1. **Continuous documentation**: Update weekly, not retroactively
2. **Multiple evidence types**: Technical, leadership, mentoring, feedback
3. **Quantified impact**: Metrics, before/after comparisons, business value
4. **Broad scope**: Team, cross-team, and organizational contributions
5. **Artifact preservation**: Links to docs, code, presentations, and feedback

**Key Takeaway**: Start your brag document today. Spend 15 minutes each week capturing your impact. When promotion time arrives, you'll have a comprehensive evidence base instead of struggling to remember what you did.
