# Mid to Senior Promotion

> **Core skill:** Building the evidence-backed case for promotion from mid-level to senior: sustained ownership, independent judgment, and team impact demonstrated over time, not a single heroic project.

## What Changes from Mid to Senior

A mid-level engineer delivers features reliably with moderate supervision. A senior engineer owns problems end to end: framing the problem before solving it, making and defending design decisions without being asked, unblocking teammates, and being the person others trust with the system's risky corners.

The promotion case is not "I did senior work once". It is "I have been operating at senior level consistently, and here is the evidence from the last two or more quarters".

| Dimension | Mid-level expectation | Senior expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Owns tasks and features | Owns a system area and its outcomes |
| Autonomy | Executes agreed plans; escalates surprises | Frames problems, proposes plans, handles surprises |
| Ambiguity | Reduces ambiguity inside a task | Finds and resolves ambiguity before it becomes rework |
| Impact | Delivers assigned work well | Changes how the team works; multiplies others |
| Communication | Reports status clearly | Explains trade-offs; influences decisions without authority |

## What Promotion Committees Actually Evaluate

Most engineering ladders (and this vault's [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/05_Career_Ladders|Career Ladders]] note) assess senior promotion on five recurring questions:

1. **Sustained performance.** Has the behavior been consistent for two or more quarters, or is it a one-off? Committees discount spikes.
2. **Ownership without prompting.** Did problems get found, framed, and solved because this engineer noticed them, or because a manager assigned them?
3. **Judgment quality.** Were design and trade-off decisions sound, documented, and defensible under challenge?
4. **Scope of impact.** Did the work improve only the engineer's own output, or the team's delivery, quality, or shared understanding?
5. **Communication and trust.** Do peers, cross-functional partners, and leads describe the engineer as someone they rely on?

Each question maps to evidence types in [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/02_Evidence_Collection|Evidence Collection]] and quantification guidance in [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/03_Impact_Quantification|Impact Quantification]].

## The Mid to Senior Delta Template

Fill this in per candidate cycle. One row per capability with a concrete example and its evidence artifact.

```markdown
| Capability | My mid-level baseline | My current senior-level behavior | Evidence artifact | Since (date) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | e.g. completed assigned tickets | e.g. own the payments service backlog end to end | link to backlog/runbook | YYYY-MM |
| Problem framing | e.g. implemented given specs | e.g. rewrote the problem statement for X; scope cut 30% | link to problem doc | YYYY-MM |
| Design judgment | e.g. followed existing patterns | e.g. ADR for Y with 3 options and trade-offs | link to ADR | YYYY-MM |
| Quality and reliability | e.g. wrote tests for my code | e.g. added regression gate that caught 2 bad releases | link to pipeline | YYYY-MM |
| Team impact | e.g. reviewed PRs when asked | e.g. review SLA habit; mentored 1 joiner through ramp | link to mentoring notes | YYYY-MM |
```

## Common Reasons for "Not Yet" (and the Fix)

| Reason committees give | What it really means | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| "Impact was scoped to your own tasks" | No evidence of system-area or team-level ownership | Volunteer to own a component's backlog, incidents, or quality metrics |
| "Inconsistent over the period" | Spikes of senior behavior, not a baseline | Pick two capabilities and make them habits, tracked weekly |
| "We saw execution, not judgment" | Decisions were made elsewhere; you implemented them | Write down decisions you made and why; seek design-review ownership |
| "Communication was status-only" | No evidence of influencing decisions or unblocking others | Run a design review; write the problem doc; coach a peer through a blocker |
| "Single big project, hard to separate from the team" | Attributable individual contribution is unclear | Use the artifact trail: your ADRs, your reviews, your postmortem actions |

## Practical Exercise

Run a mock promotion cycle on yourself:
1. Re-read your level's expectations in [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/04_Self_Assessment|Self Assessment]] and rate yourself 1–5 per capability
2. For each of the five committee questions above, write one paragraph of concrete evidence with links
3. Fill in the delta template above; mark every cell with no artifact as "claim without evidence"
4. Ask two peers and your manager: "What would you need to see to consider me senior in the next two quarters?" and record the answers verbatim
5. Choose the two weakest cells and define a 90-day plan to produce artifacts for them (see [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/06_Capstone_Project|Capstone Project]])
6. Schedule a quarterly self-review to re-run steps 1–3

## Look Ahead: This Evidence Also Serves the Staff Case

The evidence mechanics in this module — sustained scope, attributable artifacts, quantified impact — carry forward to the senior-to-staff case. What changes is the *scale* of the claims (cross-team vs team) and the *time horizon* (multiple quarters of org-level influence). When that time comes, this note's delta template becomes a scope-upgrade template; see [[career-path/03_Staff_Engineer/00_overview|Staff Engineer]] and [[career-path/02_Senior_Software_Engineer/05_Career_Ladders|Career Ladders]] for the staff-level delta.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the promotion cycle to start collecting evidence | Evidence is stale or missing when the window opens | Collect continuously; the brag document writes itself |
| Claiming team outcomes as personal impact | Committees probe attribution and trust is lost | Attribute honestly: "I led X with Y's support" |
| One giant project as the whole case | Spikes are discounted; sustainment is unproven | Show the same behavior across at least three contexts |
| Arguing effort and hours | Ladders measure scope and impact, not input | Convert every effort claim into an outcome claim |
| Ignoring the "not yet" feedback | The same reason returns next cycle | Map each past reason to one new artifact before re-applying |

## Success Indicators

- Every promotion claim you make has a linked artifact behind it within thirty seconds of being asked
- Your manager and at least two peers independently describe you as operating at senior level
- The delta template shows sustained senior behavior for two or more consecutive quarters
- Past "not yet" reasons are each answered by specific new evidence
- You can state your case in five minutes without notes and without overselling
