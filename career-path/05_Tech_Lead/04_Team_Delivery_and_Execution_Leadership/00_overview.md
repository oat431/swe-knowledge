# Team Delivery and Execution Leadership

> **Core capability:** The tech lead keeps the team's work moving — planned honestly, unblocked quickly, coordinated across teams, and landing in production safely.

## Why This Matters

Delivery is where technical leadership meets promises. The tech lead is the person who can see both the technical reality (what the work actually involves) and the delivery commitment (what has been promised) — and keeps the two in honest contact.

Teams without delivery leadership overcommit, discover blockers late, and coordinate by accident. The tech lead adds a delivery system on top of individual execution: planning that reflects reality, a visible blocker culture, risk registers that get read, and release paths that are boring.

## The Delivery Leadership Loop

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    PLAN["Plan honestly"] --> EXECUTE["Execute with visibility"]
    EXECUTE --> UNBLOCK["Unblock fast"]
    UNBLOCK --> TRACK["Track risks and drift"]
    TRACK --> LAND["Land safely"]
    LAND --> LEARN["Measure and learn"]
    LEARN --> PLAN
```

## What Changes from Senior to Tech Lead

| Activity | Senior engineer | Tech lead |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Estimation | Estimates own work | Leads the team's estimation practice and its honesty |
| Blockers | Escalates own blockers | Runs the culture in which blockers surface early |
| Risk | Manages risks in own area | Owns the team's delivery risk picture |
| Coordination | Coordinates own touchpoints | Owns the team's cross-team commitments |
| Releases | Ships own changes safely | Designs the release path everyone ships through |
| Reporting | Reports status | Tells stakeholders what the signals mean |

## Practical Applications

### Delivery Leadership Checklist

- [ ] Plans are built from capacity evidence, with buffers and leave accounted
- [ ] Estimation practice guards against anchoring and false precision
- [ ] Blockers surface daily and have a resolution path per type
- [ ] A delivery risk register exists, is read, and is updated mid-cycle
- [ ] Cross-team commitments have named counterpart leads and sync cadence
- [ ] Release criteria are defined; rollbacks are designed, not improvised
- [ ] Delivery health is reviewed from flow metrics, never individual metrics

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Optimism as planning** | Commitments become fiction; trust erodes | Plan with the team, from evidence, with buffers |
| **Heroic unblocking** | You become the blocker-processor; nothing moves without you | Fix the conditions, teach the unblocking |
| **Invisible risks** | Surprises are announced, not discovered | A living risk register someone actually reads |
| **Coordination by goodwill** | Cross-team work slips silently | Explicit contracts, owners, and sync cadence |

## Success Indicators

- Commitments are met or renegotiated early — rarely broken silently
- Blockers surface within a day, not within a sprint
- Stakeholders trust the team's "no" because its "yes" is reliable
- Releases are routine; nobody schedules adrenaline

## Summary

Delivery leadership is a system: honest planning, fast unblocking, visible risk, explicit coordination, and boring releases. The tech lead runs the system so the team can run the work — and uses delivery itself as the training ground for growing engineers.
