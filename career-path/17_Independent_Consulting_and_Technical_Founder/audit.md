# Audit: Independent Consulting and Technical Founder

> **Verdict:** An excellent, honest guide to running a **one-person technical firm**. It covers discovery and willingness-to-pay tests, offers and pricing, runway math, SOWs and change orders, managing several clients, "quality without a safety net", right-sized architecture, selling ethically, contracts, tax rhythm, insurance, and solo continuity planning. The **"Technical Founder" half of the title is under-served**: co-founders and equity, fundraising mechanics, product-market fit, hiring the first team, and acting as a startup CTO each get 0–2 notes. It also has **no defined exits** (`next_paths` is empty) and cites **none of the classic sources**.
>
> **Overall: 3.5 / 5**

## ✅ What's Good

- **Validation before building.** An evidence ladder and commitment tests for willingness to pay, "reading a soft answer", and problem interviews without pitching.
- **Honest economics.** True monthly cost baseline, runway mathematics, pipeline math, unit economics with a worked example, expected value "for a firm of one", and staging with kill criteria.
- **Delivery written for a solo operator.** The 60 percent rule, the swap principle for scope change, margin warning signs, self-review rituals ("automation as your reviewer"), and a handover package with a referral ask.
- **Architecture for one maintainer.** Complexity budgets, a boring-technology policy, cost guardrails, "scale by subtraction before addition", and maintaining systems you sold.
- **Professional responsibility.** Ethical selling, insurance as risk transfer, client due diligence on data custody, a one-page continuity plan, and an ethics compass. The "orientation, not advice — confirm locally" disclaimers are the right tone.

## ❌ What's Missing

1. **The technical-founder journey.** Thin or missing:
   - Co-founder selection, equity splits, and vesting (2 notes)
   - Fundraising mechanics: SAFEs, priced rounds, cap tables, dilution, investor relations (2 notes; `05_Bootstrapping_vs_Funding` is the only real treatment)
   - Product-market fit measurement: retention curves, the "very disappointed" survey (1 note)
   - Hiring the first engineers and setting early culture (5 passing mentions)
   - Being the startup CTO: speed-vs-debt policy, scaling from 1 to 10 engineers, when to hire a VP Eng (0 notes)
2. **AI as leverage and as a product risk (2 notes).** In 2026: AI-augmented one-person companies (what to automate in delivery, sales, and admin), the risks of AI-wrapper products (model cost and platform dependency), and AI consulting as a service line.
3. **Exits and re-entry.** `next_paths` is empty. No note covers selling a practice or product, acqui-hires, or returning to employment and how independent experience maps to Staff/EM/PM interviews.
4. **Stages of independence.** The progression diagram (consult → offer → business → company) is never expanded into a staged guide: side income → freelance → productized service → product → company, with signals for each step.
5. **Founder wellbeing.** Isolation, income volatility stress, and burnout appear in 5 notes in passing. Solo operators are the most exposed people in the map.
6. **Home-jurisdiction guidance.** The governance notes are rightly generic, and Thailand appears as the example of foreign-ownership rules for non-residents. If you operate *from* Thailand, a local appendix would be practical: business form choices, VAT registration, withholding tax on service fees, PDPA obligations, and the filing calendar, all checked with a local accountant.

## ⚠️ What to Improve

- **Credit sources** (see below), especially for the discovery, pricing, and positioning material.
