# Product vs Service Decisions

> **Core skill:** Deciding what to sell across the spectrum from bespoke services to scalable products — and how the mix evolves as capacity, cash flow, and appetite change.

## Why This Matters

Independent work spans a spectrum: bespoke consulting at one end, a product business at the other, with several hybrids between. Moving right promises leverage and scale; it also trades cash today for cash later, replaces client risk with market risk, and adds maintenance, support, and go-to-market obligations that do not exist for pure services. Choosing a point on the spectrum — explicitly, and revisably — is a founder decision, not a drift.

The economics differ in shape, not just size. Services pay now and scale with hours; products pay later and scale with users, carrying support and churn costs forever after. Services concentrate risk in a few clients; products concentrate it in market fit and build cost. Services give immediate feedback from real buyers; products give feedback through funnels and telemetry. Neither is better in the abstract — but mismatching the model to available cash, capacity, and appetite is how independents end up with a product nobody needs or a service career that never compounds.

The usual failure is jumping too early: building a product to escape client work before the same problem has been solved, repeatedly, for money. The reliable path runs the other way: sell a service, notice what repeats, productize the repetition — templates, fixed-scope offers, tooling — and only then consider an actual product. Each step right should be earned by repetition and demand, not by ambition or fatigue.

## Service vs Product Economics

| Dimension | Services | Products |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Cash flow | Paid as delivered, near-term | Invested first, paid after fit |
| Scalability | Capped by hours and people | Decoupled from hours |
| Margin shape | High per hour, flat per year | Thin early, compounding late |
| Market risk | Client concentration | Fit, churn, competition |
| Execution risk | Delivery quality | Build, security, support, billing |
| Feedback speed | Immediate and conversational | Indirect and statistical |
| Core asset | Reputation and relationships | Code, brand, distribution |
| Failure cost | Wasted hours | Wasted months plus maintenance |

## Hybrid Models

| Model | Description | Fits When |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Consulting plus templates | Services funded by reusable toolkits | Repetition is visible in every engagement |
| Productized service | Fixed-scope, fixed-price delivery of one workflow | The same solution sells three or more times |
| Service-first product | Client work funds and shapes a product | A client pays for the first version |
| Open-core | Free tooling with paid support or enterprise layer | Distribution comes from developer adoption |
| Content-led hybrid | Audience and courses feed services and products | You can teach the space credibly |
| Maintenance products | Retainers for keeping delivered systems healthy | Clients lack the skills to run what was built |

## Decision Triggers

| Signal | What It Suggests | Move |
|--------|------------------|------|
| Same problem solved three or more times | Repetition exists | Productize the service packaging first |
| Demand exceeds delivery capacity | Constraint is you | Raise price or productize before hiring |
| Clients ask for the tool, not the hours | Appetite for a product | Validate with a paid pilot, not a build |
| Maintenance requests recur post-delivery | Recurring revenue hiding in plain sight | Offer a maintenance retainer |
| Boredom with delivery | Fatigue, not strategy | Change the offer mix, not the business model |

## The Model Ladder


Movement along the ladder is funded by what the previous step earned: cash, proof, and repetition. Skipping steps is how builders spend their savings on maintenance they never priced.

## Practical Applications

### Mix Review Checklist

- [ ] Revenue by stream is visible and tracked quarterly
- [ ] The most repeated delivery tasks are named as productization candidates
- [ ] Any product ambition is attached to evidence from paying users
- [ ] Support and maintenance costs are estimated before any product build
- [ ] Capacity risk is monitored: what happens if two clients escalate at once?
- [ ] The current mix matches available cash and personal appetite for risk

### Quarterly Mix Review Template


## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Product as escape hatch** | Products need sales, support, and marketing too | Validate a paid pilot before committing to build |
| **Productizing too early** | No repetition means no product, just a configurable service | Wait for the third sale of the same solution |
| **Ignoring maintenance** | Every shipped product accrues support and upgrade debt | Price support in from the first version |
| **Splitting focus** | One person running two businesses delivers neither | Sequence the moves; one bet per quarter |
| **Client tooling as product** | Built for one client's assumptions, unsellable elsewhere | Generalize only after two independent requests |
| **Underpricing the hybrid** | Bundles hide effort and eat the service margin | Price components separately even when sold together |

## Success Indicators

- There is a one-sentence answer to "what do you sell?" that matches the invoices
- Productization decisions cite repetition and revenue, not enthusiasm
- Each revenue stream has a visible cost of delivery
- No zombie products: every product has users, revenue, or a kill date
- The mix changes deliberately, at a review, not by accident

## Summary

Product versus service decisions define what the independent business actually is: a point on a spectrum between bespoke work and scalable products, each with its own cash flow, risk, and capability demands. The durable path moves right only on evidence — repetition, demand, and paying users — so the mix evolves as a series of funded, deliberate steps rather than a leap away from the clients who pay the bills.
