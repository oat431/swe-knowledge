# Problem Interviews Without Pitching

> **Core skill:** Running discovery conversations that extract facts about the prospect's past and present — avoiding pitches, hypotheticals, and leading questions that contaminate the signal.

## Why This Matters

The interview is the instrument of discovery, and a contaminated instrument is worse than none because it produces data that feels like progress. Pitching mid-interview, eagerly validating every complaint, and asking about the future all generate polite fiction: the prospect tells you what keeps the conversation pleasant, and you write it down as market evidence.

The antidote is a small set of rules applied with discipline. Talk about their life, not your idea. Ask about facts — what happened, what it cost, what they did next — not opinions, predictions, or preferences. Treat compliments as noise and commitments as signal. These rules are borrowed from customer development practice and they work precisely because they are uncomfortable: most conversations drift back toward pitching within three minutes.

For an independent, every discovery interview is also a relationship — which raises the stakes. Done well, the interview demonstrates competence without selling: sharp questions, no posturing, and a next step that respects their time. Done badly, it teaches the prospect that you are like every other vendor, and the warmest person in your network goes quiet.

## Question Quality

| Theme | Weak Question | Strong Question |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Hypotheticals | "Would you use a tool that does X?" | "How did you handle X the last time it happened?" |
| Compliments | "Do you like the idea?" | "What have you already tried to fix this?" |
| Future promises | "Would you pay for this?" | "What did this problem cost you last quarter?" |
| Leading | "Isn't the reporting side frustrating?" | "Walk me through the reporting process end to end" |
| Idea ranking | "Would this or option B be better?" | "What is the hardest part of this workflow today?" |
| Buyer process | "Could you buy this if you wanted?" | "When you last bought something like this, how did that go?" |

Every weak question invites the prospect to imagine, oblige, or guess. Every strong question asks them to report a fact they already know.

## Interview Arc

| Phase | Goal | Sample Question |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| Context | Understand their role, tools, and cadence | "What does a normal week look like for your team?" |
| Problem | Watch the pain appear unprompted | "Tell me about the last time this broke down" |
| Cost | Quantify time, money, and risk | "What did that delay end up costing?" |
| Workaround | Map current tools, hacks, and spend | "What do you do today to keep it moving?" |
| Priority | Place this pain among their others | "Where does this rank against your other fires?" |
| Decision | Find the trigger and the signer | "Who else would weigh in if this got fixed?" |
| Close | Agree the next step | "Can I share a short idea and get your reaction next week?" |

The arc moves from their world toward your hypothesis — never the reverse. If the prospect asks what you do, answer in one sentence and return the question.

## The Interview Discipline

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    CONTEXT["Their context and role"] --> PROBLEM["Problem in their words"]
    PROBLEM --> COST["Cost of the problem"]
    COST --> WORKAROUND["Current workaround and spend"]
    WORKAROUND --> CLOSE["Agreed next step"]
```

The discipline is in the sequence: facts before interpretation, cost before solution, and a next step before the conversation ends. Skipping to the solution collapses the interview into a pitch.

## Practical Applications

### Interviewer Checklist

- [ ] The interview opens with context questions, not the idea
- [ ] At least three questions ask about the past rather than the future
- [ ] The prospect talks more than eighty percent of the time
- [ ] Numbers are collected: hours, money, failure rates, budgets
- [ ] The idea is described only if the prospect explicitly asks
- [ ] A specific next step with a date is agreed before hanging up

### Discovery Interview Script

```markdown
## Interview Script — <prospect, role>

1. What does your role own, and what does a normal week look like?
2. Tell me about the last time <problem area> caused trouble.
3. What did that cost — time, money, or something else?
4. What do you do today to work around it?
5. What have you already tried? What happened?
6. Where does this rank against everything else on your plate?
7. If it got fixed, who else would need to agree?
8. Is there anyone else I should talk to about this?
```

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Pitching mid-interview** | The pitch ends their openness and starts their politeness | Hold the idea; offer it in a separate follow-up |
| **Hypothetical questions** | Predictions about the future cost the prospect nothing | Ask for recent, concrete, lived examples |
| **Accepting compliments** | "Great idea" is social lubricant, not evidence | Redirect to what they spent and tried |
| **Filling their silence** | The rescuer learns nothing; the rescued relaxes | Wait three seconds; let them finish the thought |
| **One script for everyone** | Roles differ; you compare answers that cannot compare | Adapt the arc per role; keep the discipline identical |
| **No logging within a day** | Memory rewrites quotes into your expectations | Write the log before the next conversation |

## Success Indicators

- Prospects volunteer the same problem before you ask about it
- Logs contain verbatim quotes and numbers rather than impressions
- You can predict a prospect's answers by the third interview in a segment
- Prospects ask what you do and pull the follow-up themselves
- Conversations end with a scheduled next step more often than not

## Summary

Problem interviews without pitching are a discipline of restraint: ask about past and present facts, quantify the cost, map the workaround, and let the prospect pull the idea out of you rather than the reverse. The independent who masters this converts conversations into trustworthy market evidence — and, incidentally, into the most credible sales channel they will ever have.
