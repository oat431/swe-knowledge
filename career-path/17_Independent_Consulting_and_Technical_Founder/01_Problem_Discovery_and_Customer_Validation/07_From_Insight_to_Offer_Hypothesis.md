# From Insight to Offer Hypothesis

> **Core skill:** Converting discovery patterns into a testable offer hypothesis — who pays, for what outcome, at what price — with a cheap test and a kill criterion attached.

## Why This Matters

Insights do not sell; offers do. The bridge between them is a hypothesis specific enough to be proved wrong. "Small businesses struggle with data" is a direction, not a hypothesis: it cannot be priced, sold, or killed. The independent who converts observations into sharp, testable claims escapes the fog where interesting conversations replace income.

A testable hypothesis has anatomy: a named buyer, a trigger event, a promised outcome, a price expectation, and a vehicle — audit, project, pilot, or product. Attached to it are two more parts most independents omit: the cheapest test that could fail, and the kill criterion that decides when it has. Those two parts are what make the hypothesis an instrument rather than a wish.

The working loop is short: pattern, hypothesis, test, decision. Early on, several hypothesis cards run in parallel at low cost; as evidence accumulates, the portfolio narrows and the survivor gets the build. The discipline is identical to product discovery, applied to an offer — and it continues after launch, because offers drift out of fit as markets move.

## Anatomy of a Testable Hypothesis

| Element | Question It Answers | Example |
|---------|--------------------|---------|
| Buyer | Who signs and who feels the pain? | VP Engineering at 50 to 200 person SaaS companies |
| Trigger | What event makes this urgent now? | A platform migration or compliance deadline |
| Outcome | What measurable change do they get? | Migration completed without customer-visible downtime |
| Vehicle | What form does the offer take? | Two-week paid assessment, then fixed-scope migration |
| Price signal | What will they pay, and on what model? | Flat fee, benchmarked against a quarter of internal effort |
| Test | What is the cheapest way to verify? | Three paid assessments sold within six weeks |
| Kill criterion | What result ends this line of thinking? | No paid assessment after twenty conversations |

Fill every row or the hypothesis is not yet testable — it is still a mood.

## From Observation to Hypothesis

| Raw Observation | Pattern | Testable Hypothesis |
|-----------------|---------|---------------------|
| "Onboarding new data sources takes weeks" | Onboarding is the sustained pain, not the tool choice | A two-week onboarding assessment at a fixed fee sells when a platform goes live |
| "Everyone tracks renewals in spreadsheets" | Ops teams trust no existing tooling for this workflow | A renewal-risk report sold monthly to RevOps leads earns retainer revenue |
| "Budget appears in Q4" | Buying is cyclical and budget-driven | Outreach timed to Q3 produces the highest paid-pilot conversion |

The middle column is the intellectual work: compress many conversations into one pattern. The right column is the commercial bet: a form, a buyer, and a price that can be falsified.

## Test Selection

| Test | Cost to You | Strongest Evidence It Produces |
|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| One-page pitch to ten warm prospects | Hours | Message resonance and objection patterns |
| Sold paid assessment | Days | Budget access and real urgency |
| Concierge pilot with one client | Weeks of delivery | Whether the outcome matters enough to pay again |
| Pre-sale of a product or workshop seat | Days of setup | Demand strength before building |
| Limited-seat cohort or sprint | Weeks | Price tolerance and repeatability |

Choose the cheapest test that could still fail. A test that cannot fail is marketing, not validation.

## The Hypothesis Loop

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    INSIGHT["Discovery insights"] --> PATTERN["Repeating pattern"]
    PATTERN --> HYPOTHESIS["Offer hypothesis"]
    HYPOTHESIS --> TEST["Cheapest test that can fail"]
    TEST --> DECIDE["Refine, pivot, or proceed"]
```

Each turn of the loop costs less than a month and produces a decision. Proceeding without a test is the expensive alternative the loop exists to prevent.

## Practical Applications

### Hypothesis Hygiene Checklist

- [ ] The buyer, trigger, and outcome fit in three sentences
- [ ] A price expectation is stated, even roughly, before testing
- [ ] The test is the cheapest one that could genuinely fail
- [ ] Kill criteria and a decision date are written in advance
- [ ] Evidence from the test is payment, commitment, or behavior — not opinion
- [ ] At most two or three hypotheses run in parallel

### Offer Hypothesis Card

```markdown
## Offer Hypothesis — <short name>

| Field | Entry |
|-------|-------|
| Buyer | <role, company type> |
| Trigger | <event that creates urgency> |
| Outcome promise | <measurable change> |
| Vehicle | <assessment, project, pilot, product> |
| Price expectation | <model and range> |
| Test | <cheapest test that can fail> |
| Kill criterion | <result that ends this line> |
| Decision date | <date> |
```

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Vague hypothesis** | "Consulting for startups" cannot be tested or priced | Specify buyer, trigger, outcome, and price |
| **No kill criterion** | Every result gets rationalized as progress | Write the failing result before running the test |
| **Price left out** | A hypothesis without price skips the hardest question | Anchor a number or a model early |
| **Attached to the idea** | Identity fuses with the bet; evidence stops mattering | Treat cards as disposable; keep the buyer sacred |
| **Testing on friends** | Sympathy passes every test you design | Test with strangers who match the buyer role |
| **Too many hypotheses** | Parallel bets split attention and stall all of them | Two or three maximum; concentrate on the leader |

## Success Indicators

- Offers sell in some form before significant build begins
- Each test costs days or weeks, never months, and ends in a decision
- Failed hypotheses are killed on schedule rather than redecorated
- Surviving hypotheses accumulate evidence from payment, not praise
- The founder can state, per hypothesis, exactly what would change their mind

## Summary

From insight to offer hypothesis is the compression step that turns a pile of discovery notes into a commercial bet worth testing: name the buyer and trigger, promise a measurable outcome, attach a price and a vehicle, and specify the cheapest test that can fail along with the kill criterion. Run the loop cheaply and often, and the offer that survives is one the market — not the founder — selected.
