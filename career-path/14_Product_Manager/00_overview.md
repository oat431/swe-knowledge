# Product Manager

> **Positioning:** A product and business path focused on discovering valuable problems, defining product direction, and coordinating decisions that create outcomes for customers and the organization.

## What This Path Is

A Product Manager connects customer needs, business strategy, market context, design, technology, and delivery. The role owns product direction and prioritization, but does not usually manage the engineers who build the product.

A software engineering background can be a strong advantage because it helps the Product Manager understand feasibility, technical risk, system constraints, data, quality, and the cost of different choices. Technical knowledge is useful, but the center of gravity shifts from building the solution to deciding which problems deserve investment.

## Primary Outcomes

- A clear product vision and strategy
- Strong understanding of customer problems and context
- Prioritized opportunities linked to value and evidence
- Product decisions that balance desirability, viability, feasibility, and risk
- Measurable product outcomes, not only shipped features
- Alignment among product, design, engineering, operations, sales, and leadership

## Product Management and Project Management

| Dimension | Product Manager | Project Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | What should we build and why? | How will we coordinate the work? |
| Main focus | Customer, market, product value, outcomes | Scope, schedule, resources, risks, governance |
| Success measure | Adoption, value, customer and business outcomes | Delivery and agreed project outcomes |
| Time horizon | Ongoing product lifecycle | Temporary project lifecycle |
| Authority | Product direction and prioritization | Delivery coordination and project controls |

## Signals for Moving Forward

- You are more interested in which problems to solve than in implementing every solution yourself.
- You enjoy customer conversations, prioritization, experimentation, and decision framing.
- You can make choices with incomplete information and learn from outcomes.
- You can represent customer and business needs while respecting technical constraints.
- You are willing to measure success through user and business behavior rather than code quality alone.

## Evidence to Build

- Customer interview plan and research report
- Problem statement and opportunity map
- Product vision and strategy
- Product requirements document
- Prioritization model
- Product roadmap
- Experiment or A/B test plan
- Product analytics definition
- Product launch or outcome review
