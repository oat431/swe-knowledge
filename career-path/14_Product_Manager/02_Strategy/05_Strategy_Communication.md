# Strategy Communication

> Strategy only works if the organization understands it, believes in it, and acts on it. Strategy communication transforms strategic documents into shared understanding and coordinated action.

## Why Strategy Communication Matters

**Without communication:**
- Strategy sits in documents nobody reads
- Teams make decisions misaligned with strategy
- Confusion about priorities
- Strategy execution fails

**With communication:**
- Shared understanding of direction
- Aligned decision-making
- Clear priorities
- Coordinated execution

## Strategy Communication Framework

### 1. Message Hierarchy

**Level 1: Vision**
- One inspiring sentence
- The future we're creating
- Memorable and repeatable

**Level 2: Strategic Goals**
- 3-5 key outcomes
- How we'll know we're succeeding
- Measurable and time-bound

**Level 3: Strategic Choices**
- What we will and won't do
- Key trade-offs made
- Rationale for choices

**Level 4: Implications**
- What this means for each team
- Specific actions and priorities
- Decision guidelines

### 2. Audience Adaptation

**Executive leadership:**
- Focus: Strategic rationale, business impact, competitive positioning
- Format: Strategy deck, executive summary
- Frequency: Quarterly reviews

**Product and engineering:**
- Focus: Product implications, technical direction, priorities
- Format: Strategy sessions, roadmap alignment
- Frequency: Monthly updates

**Sales and marketing:**
- Focus: Market positioning, competitive messaging, target segments
- Format: Battle cards, positioning guides
- Frequency: Quarterly enablement

**Customer success:**
- Focus: Customer impact, success criteria, support priorities
- Format: Customer journey alignment
- Frequency: Monthly updates

**All employees:**
- Focus: Vision, goals, how they contribute
- Format: All-hands, internal communications
- Frequency: Quarterly all-hands

## Communication Techniques

### 1. Storytelling

**Structure:**
```
Context: Where we are now
Challenge: What we're facing
Choice: What we decided to do
Future: Where this takes us
```

**Example:**
```
Context: "We've built a solid customer service platform serving 
small businesses."

Challenge: "But the mid-market is underserved and growing, while 
enterprise competitors ignore them."

Choice: "We're focusing on mid-market companies with a simplified, 
self-service platform."

Future: "This positions us to capture a $2B market opportunity 
and become the leader in mid-market customer service."
```

### 2. Repetition

**Rule of 7:**
- People need to hear message 7 times
- Use different channels
- Vary the format
- Reinforce consistently

**Techniques:**
- Repeat in meetings
- Include in documents
- Display visually
- Reference in decisions

### 3. Two-Way Communication

**Listen:**
- Ask for questions
- Solicit feedback
- Conduct surveys
- Hold office hours

**Respond:**
- Address concerns
- Clarify confusion
- Incorporate feedback
- Acknowledge input

### 4. Connect to Daily Work

**For each team, answer:**
- How does strategy affect our work?
- What should we prioritize?
- What should we stop doing?
- How will we know we're succeeding?

**Example:**
```
Engineering:
- Focus on unified customer view (not mobile app)
- Prioritize integrations (not customization)
- Measure by call handling time reduction

Marketing:
- Target mid-market (not enterprise or SMB)
- Emphasize simplicity and speed (not features)
- Measure by mid-market lead generation
```

## Common Communication Mistakes

### 1. One-Time Communication

**Mistake:** Announce strategy once, assume understood
**Fix:** Communicate repeatedly through multiple channels

### 2. Jargon and Complexity

**Mistake:** Strategy document only leadership understands
**Fix:** Simple, clear language everyone can repeat

### 3. Abstract Without Concrete

**Mistake:** High-level vision without implications
**Fix:** Connect strategy to specific actions and decisions

### 4. Top-Down Only

**Mistake:** Announce without listening
**Fix:** Two-way communication, address concerns

### 5. Inconsistent Messages

**Mistake:** Different leaders say different things
**Fix:** Aligned leadership, consistent messaging

## Measuring Communication Effectiveness

### Surveys

**Strategy recall:**
- Can you state our product vision?
- What are our 3 strategic goals?
- What are our key strategic choices?

**Strategy clarity:**
- Do you understand how strategy affects your work?
- Are priorities clear?
- Can you make decisions aligned with strategy?

**Strategy belief:**
- Do you believe in our strategy?
- Do you think it will succeed?
- Are you committed to executing it?

### Behavioral Indicators

**Decision alignment:**
- Are decisions consistent with strategy?
- Do teams prioritize strategically?
- Are resources allocated to strategic priorities?

**Execution progress:**
- Are strategic initiatives progressing?
- Are we achieving strategic goals?
- Is strategy translating to action?

## Senior-Level Strategy Communication

1. **Inspire with strategy**
   - Not just inform
   - Build excitement and commitment
   - Connect to purpose

2. **Enable strategic decisions**
   - Provide decision frameworks
   - Empower teams to decide
   - Build strategic thinking capability

3. **Build communication systems**
   - Not just one-time communication
   - Ongoing communication processes
   - Feedback loops

4. **Model strategic alignment**
   - Make decisions aligned with strategy
   - Reference strategy in communications
   - Reinforce strategic behaviors

## Metrics
