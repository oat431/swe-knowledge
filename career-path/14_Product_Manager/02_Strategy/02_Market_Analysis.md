# Market Analysis

> Market analysis provides the external context for product strategy. It reveals opportunities, threats, trends, and dynamics that shape strategic choices.

## Why Market Analysis Matters

**Without market analysis:**
- Building in a vacuum
- Missing market shifts
- Misjudging opportunity size
- Reactive to competitors

**With market analysis:**
- Informed strategic choices
- Anticipating market changes
- Identifying underserved segments
- Proactive positioning

## Market Analysis Framework

### 1. Market Sizing

**Total Addressable Market (TAM):**
- Entire revenue opportunity if you captured 100%
- "If every potential customer bought our product"

**Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM):**
- Portion of TAM you can realistically serve
- Based on your business model, geography, channels

**Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM):**
- Portion of SAM you can realistically capture
- Based on competition, resources, go-to-market

**Example:**
```
TAM: $10B global customer service software market
SAM: $2B mid-market segment (100-1000 employees)
SOM: $200M achievable in 3 years with current resources
```

**Methods:**
- Top-down: Industry reports, analyst data
- Bottom-up: Customer count × average revenue
- Value-based: Value created × capture rate

### 2. Market Segmentation

**Divide market into meaningful groups:**

**Demographic:**
- Company size (employees, revenue)
- Industry vertical
- Geography
- Company age

**Behavioral:**
- Usage patterns
- Purchase behavior
- Loyalty
- Readiness to buy

**Needs-based:**
- Problems they're solving
- Jobs to be done
- Pain points
- Desired outcomes

**Example:**
```
Segment 1: High-Volume Support Teams
- 50+ agents, 500+ calls/day
- Need: Efficiency and automation
- Willing to pay: Premium for time savings

Segment 2: Growing Support Teams
- 10-50 agents, scaling rapidly
- Need: Scalable processes and training
- Willing to pay: For growth enablement

Segment 3: Cost-Conscious Teams
- <20 agents, budget constrained
- Need: Basic functionality at low cost
- Willing to pay: Only for essentials
```

### 3. Market Trends

**Identify forces shaping the market:**

**Technology trends:**
- AI and automation
- Cloud adoption
- Mobile-first
- Integration expectations

**Customer trends:**
- Rising expectations
- Self-service preference
- Personalization demand
- Privacy concerns

**Business trends:**
- Remote work
- Subscription models
- Outcome-based pricing
- Platform ecosystems

**Regulatory trends:**
- Data protection (GDPR, CCPA)
- Industry-specific regulations
- Compliance requirements

### 4. Market Dynamics

**Understand market forces:**

**Growth rate:**
- Is the market growing, stable, or declining?
- What's driving growth or decline?

**Seasonality:**
- Are there seasonal patterns?
- How do they affect demand?

**Cyclicality:**
- Is the market tied to economic cycles?
- How recession-proof is it?

**Disruption potential:**
- What could disrupt this market?
- Who might enter from adjacent markets?

## Competitive Landscape

### Competitor Analysis

**Direct competitors:**
- Solve the same problem
- Target the same customers
- Similar value proposition

**Indirect competitors:**
- Solve the problem differently
- Alternative approaches
- Adjacent solutions

**Potential competitors:**
- Large platforms that could enter
- Adjacent market players
- Startups in stealth mode

**Competitor profile:**
```
Competitor: [Name]
Product: [What they offer]

```

## Market Opportunity Assessment

### Opportunity Evaluation

**For each opportunity, assess:**

| Criteria | Questions | Score |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Market size | How big is the opportunity? | 1-5 |
| Growth rate | Is it growing or declining? | 1-5 |
| Competition | How crowded is it? | 1-5 |
| Fit | Does it match our capabilities? | 1-5 |
| Timing | Is the market ready? | 1-5 |

**Opportunity matrix:**
```
High attractiveness + High fit = Pursue aggressively
High attractiveness + Low fit = Partner or build capability
Low attractiveness + High fit = Consider if strategic
Low attractiveness + Low fit = Avoid
```

### Market Entry Strategies

**1. First mover**
- Enter new market first
- Establish brand and lock-in
- High risk, high reward

**2. Fast follower**
- Learn from first mover
- Enter with better product
- Lower risk, proven market

**3. Niche focus**
- Serve underserved segment
- Build expertise and loyalty
- Defend against broad competitors

**4. Disruption**
- Enter with different model
- Serve overserved customers
- Change the rules

## Market Research Methods

### Primary Research

**Customer interviews:**
- Understand needs and behaviors
- Validate assumptions
- 10-20 interviews per segment

**Surveys:**
- Quantify findings
- Measure attitudes
- 100+ responses for statistical significance

**Focus groups:**
- Explore group dynamics
- Test concepts
- 6-10 participants per group

### Secondary Research

**Industry reports:**
- Gartner, Forrester, IDC
- Market size and trends
- Competitive landscape

**Public data:**
- Government statistics
- Industry associations
- Academic research

**Competitor analysis:**
- Product features and pricing
- Marketing and positioning
- Customer reviews

## Common Market Analysis Mistakes

### 1. Overestimating Market Size

**Mistake:** "Everyone needs this"
**Fix:** Be specific about target segments

### 2. Ignoring Market Dynamics

**Mistake:** Static view of market
**Fix:** Consider trends, cycles, disruptions

### 3. Focusing Only on Direct Competitors

**Mistake:** Missing indirect threats
**Fix:** Map full competitive landscape

### 4. Analysis Paralysis

**Mistake:** Endless research without decisions
**Fix:** Time-box research, accept uncertainty

### 5. Confirmation Bias

**Mistake:** Seeking data that supports hypothesis
**Fix:** Actively seek disconfirming evidence

## Senior-Level Market Analysis

1. **Identify market opportunities**
   - Not just analyze existing markets
   - Spot emerging opportunities
   - Anticipate market shifts

2. **Shape market perception**
   - Influence analyst views
   - Define market categories
   - Thought leadership

3. **Build market intelligence capability**
   - Systematic competitive intelligence
   - Market monitoring systems
   - Insight sharing processes

4. **Connect market to strategy**
   - Translate market insights to decisions
   - Balance market pull with vision
   - Time market entry strategically
