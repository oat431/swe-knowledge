# Project Audit and Compliance

> **Core skill:** Preparing for and responding to project audits — maintaining audit-ready records, managing external auditor interactions, addressing findings, and ensuring project compliance with organizational, regulatory, and contractual requirements.

## Why This Matters

Projects operate within a web of compliance obligations: organizational policies, regulatory requirements, contractual commitments, industry standards, and funding conditions. An audit is the organization's mechanism for verifying that these obligations are being met — and a project that fails an audit faces consequences that range from embarrassing to existential: findings that must be remediated, funding that is suspended, contracts that are breached, or certifications that are revoked.

The project manager does not need to be a compliance expert — but the project manager must ensure that the project is audit-ready at all times: records are complete and accessible, processes are followed and documented, and compliance obligations are met and evidenced. Audit readiness is not a pre-audit scramble; it is the natural state of a well-managed project whose configuration management, change control, and decision recording disciplines are functioning.

## Types of Project Audits

| Audit Type | Who Conducts | Focus | Frequency |
|------------|-------------|-------|-----------|
| **Internal quality audit** | Organizational quality function | Process adherence, deliverable quality | Scheduled; per phase or annually |
| **Internal financial audit** | Internal audit department | Budget management, procurement compliance, cost reporting | Scheduled; annually or at closure |
| **External financial audit** | External auditors | Financial statements, funding compliance | Statutory schedule |
| **Regulatory audit** | Government agency, industry regulator | Regulatory compliance (safety, data, environmental, etc.) | Per regulatory schedule |
| **Customer audit** | Customer's audit team | Contractual compliance, deliverable quality, data handling | Per contract |
| **Certification audit** | Certification body (ISO, SOC2, etc.) | Standards compliance | Per certification cycle |
| **Phase gate audit** | Governance / PMO | Project management process adherence | Per phase gate |

## Audit Readiness

Audit readiness is not a pre-audit activity — it is the project's steady state:

| Readiness Dimension | What It Means | Supporting Discipline |
|---------------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Records are complete** | Every decision, change, and approval is documented | Decision recording, change control |
| **Records are accessible** | Auditors can find what they need without the PM's help | Configuration management, single repository |
| **Processes are evidenced** | The project can show it followed its defined processes | Process documentation, audit trail |
| **Compliance is demonstrated** | Obligations are met and evidenced | Compliance register, evidence collection |
| **Deviations are explained** | Any process deviations are documented with rationale and approval | Exception logging, waiver documentation |

### The Audit-Ready Project State

| Artifact | What Auditors Check | Evidence |
|----------|--------------------|----------|
| Change log | Were changes assessed and approved? | Complete change log with impact assessment and decision |
| Decision log | Were key decisions made by the right authority? | Decision records with decision-maker and rationale |
| Risk register | Were risks managed per the risk management plan? | Risk register with assessments, responses, and reviews |
| Financial records | Was budget managed within authority? | Cost reports, procurement records, invoice approvals |
| Contract records | Were contractual obligations met? | Contract, change orders, deliverable acceptance |
| Quality records | Were quality processes followed? | Test results, review records, acceptance sign-offs |
| Stakeholder communications | Were stakeholders engaged per the plan? | Communication records, meeting minutes |

## Managing an Audit

### Before the Audit

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Understand the scope** | What is being audited, against what criteria, over what period |
| **Identify the auditor's needs** | What evidence will they request? Prepare it in advance |
| **Brief the team** | Who will interact with the auditor; what to say and what not to say |
| **Organize the evidence** | Index the evidence by audit criterion so it is findable |
| **Identify known gaps** | Document any known compliance gaps with explanation and remediation plan |

### During the Audit

| Principle | Practice |
|-----------|----------|
| **Answer the question asked** | Provide the evidence requested; do not volunteer unrelated information |
| **Be honest about gaps** | If something is missing, say so — with explanation and remediation plan |
| **Do not speculate** | "I don't know, but I will find out and provide it by [time]" beats guessing |
| **Escalate appropriately** | If the auditor's request exceeds the PM's authority, escalate to the sponsor |
| **Keep a log** | Record every request, every response, and every commitment made |

### After the Audit

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Review findings** | Understand each finding: what is the gap, what is the required remediation |
| **Respond to the report** | Formal management response: agree, partially agree, or disagree with rationale |
| **Create a remediation plan** | For each finding: action, owner, deadline, evidence of closure |
| **Implement remediation** | Close findings; retain evidence |
| **Improve the process** | If the finding reveals a process weakness, fix the process — not just the finding |

## The Compliance Register

A simple tool that tracks every compliance obligation and its status:

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Obligation ID | Unique identifier |
| Source | Policy, regulation, contract, standard — with reference |
| Requirement | What must be done or evidenced |
| Owner | Who is responsible for compliance |
| Evidence | What demonstrates compliance |
| Status | Compliant / At risk / Non-compliant |
| Last verified | Date of last compliance check |
| Next review | Date of next scheduled review |
| Notes | Deviations, waivers, remediation status |

## Compliance in Programs

Program compliance adds breadth:

- **Program-level obligations**: regulatory, contractual, and organizational requirements that apply across all projects
- **Project-level obligations**: managed by project managers; monitored by the program manager for consistency
- **Cross-project compliance**: where a compliance obligation spans projects (e.g., data handling across integrated systems), the program manager ensures end-to-end compliance
- **Program audits**: may audit the program as a whole or sample projects within the program

## Practical Applications

### Audit Readiness Checklist

- [ ] Change log, decision log, risk register, and issue register are current and complete
- [ ] Financial records are reconciled and accessible
- [ ] Compliance register is maintained with status for each obligation
- [ ] Known compliance gaps are documented with remediation plans
- [ ] Evidence is organized and findable — not dependent on the PM's memory
- [ ] The team knows how to interact with auditors

### Audit Response Template

```markdown
## Audit Finding Response — Finding #[ID]
- Finding: [Auditor's description of the gap]
- Management response: [Agree / Partially agree / Disagree]
- Rationale: [If disagreeing, why; if agreeing, root cause]
- Remediation: [Action to close the finding]
- Owner: [Name]
- Deadline: [Date]
- Evidence of closure: [What will demonstrate the finding is closed]
```

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Pre-audit scramble** | Evidence assembled in a panic; gaps are discovered during the audit | Audit-ready as steady state; evidence organized continuously |
| **Defensive posture** | The PM treats the auditor as an adversary; information is guarded | Auditor as validator; honest and cooperative |
| **Finding-as-failure** | Findings are treated as personal failure; remediation is begrudged | Findings as improvement opportunities; remediation embraced |
| **Remediation without root cause** | Findings are closed but the process weakness remains | Root cause analysis; process fix, not just finding fix |
| **Evidence-by-memory** | Only the PM knows where evidence is; audit stalls when PM is unavailable | Evidence organized and indexed; accessible without the PM |

## Success Indicators

- The project can produce audit evidence within hours, not days
- Auditors' requests are met with organized evidence, not a scramble
- Audit findings result in process improvements, not just finding closure
- The compliance register is current and reviewed at governance meetings
- The project passes audits with no major findings — and minor findings are remediated on time

## Summary

Project audit and compliance is the discipline of audit readiness: maintaining complete, accessible records through functioning configuration management, change control, and decision recording; tracking compliance obligations in a register with current status and evidence; and managing audits as a professional interaction — cooperative, honest about gaps, and committed to remediation that fixes the process, not just the finding. Audit readiness is not a pre-audit scramble; it is the natural state of a well-managed project. A project that is always audit-ready spends almost no time on audits; a project that scrambles for every audit spends the same time with far more stress and far worse outcomes. The project manager who treats audit readiness as a continuous discipline gives the project an integrity that survives scrutiny — and the scrutiny will come, scheduled or not.
