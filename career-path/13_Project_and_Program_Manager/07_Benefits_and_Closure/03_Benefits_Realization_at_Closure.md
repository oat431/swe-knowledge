# Benefits Realization at Closure

> **Core skill:** Assessing actual benefits against planned benefits at project closure — honestly, with evidence — and handing the ongoing measurement responsibility to benefit owners so the organization knows what value the project actually produced.

## Why This Matters

The project closure report's benefits section is the project's value verdict: did the project produce the benefits it was chartered to produce? This is the question the sponsor, the steering committee, and the organization need answered — and it is the question the project manager is best positioned to answer.

Honesty at this moment is the project manager's final professional act on the project. A report that inflates benefits to make the project look successful is a fiction the organization will plan against — and the next project manager who inherits the inflated baseline will pay for it. A report that honestly assesses partial realization, attributes shortfalls to invalidated assumptions, and identifies benefits that require post-closure measurement gives the organization the truth it needs to plan its next investment.

## The Benefits Assessment Framework

| Assessment Dimension | Question | Evidence |
|---------------------|----------|----------|
| **Achieved** | Which planned benefits have been fully realized? | Measurement data showing target met or exceeded |
| **Partially achieved** | Which benefits have been partially realized? | Measurement data showing progress short of target |
| **Not achieved** | Which benefits have not been realized? | Measurement data showing no progress or regression |
| **Not yet measurable** | Which benefits require post-closure measurement? | Leading indicators positive; lagging measurement scheduled |
| **Invalidated** | Which benefits are no longer achievable due to changed assumptions? | Assumption change documented; benefit re-forecast to zero |
| **Unplanned** | What benefits emerged that were not in the original plan? | Evidence of positive outcomes not originally forecast |

## The Honest Benefits Statement

For each planned benefit, the closure report states:

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Benefit** | Benefit description from the benefits management plan |
| **Target** | Planned value at closure |
| **Actual** | Measured value at closure |
| **Variance** | Actual minus target — with explanation |
| **Confidence** | Confidence in the actual measurement |
| **Trajectory** | Trend: is the benefit improving, stable, or declining? |
| **Post-closure owner** | Who will continue to measure this benefit |
| **Post-closure measurement date** | When the next measurement will occur |

### Example Benefits Statement

| Benefit | Target | Actual | Variance | Explanation |
|---------|--------|--------|----------|-------------|
| Processing cost reduction | $9/invoice saved | $7/invoice saved | -$2 (78%) | Pilot showed $9; full rollout includes transition costs. Expect to reach target in 6 months |
| Support ticket reduction | -60% volume | -45% volume | -15% | Self-service adoption at 70% vs. 90% target; adoption campaign ongoing |
| Compliance: zero findings | 0 findings | 0 findings | On target | Pre-audit passed; formal audit scheduled Q2 |

## Timing the Assessment

Benefits are realized on different timescales:

| Benefit Type | Typical Realization Timeline | Assessment at Closure |
|--------------|------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Immediate** | Benefits realized as soon as the output is operational | Full assessment possible |
| **Short-term** | Benefits realized within 3 months of closure | Partial assessment; trajectory estimated |
| **Medium-term** | Benefits realized 3-12 months after closure | Leading indicators assessed; lagging measurement scheduled |
| **Long-term** | Benefits realized > 12 months after closure | Leading indicators assessed; measurement plan handed to benefit owner |

The closure report does not claim benefits that haven't been realized — it assesses what is measurable now and schedules what requires time.

## When Benefits Fall Short

The project manager's responsibility when benefits are below target:

| Action | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Explain, don't excuse** | State the variance and the root cause — factually, without defensiveness |
| **Identify what was within project control** | Did the project fail to deliver an enabler? Did scope changes remove a benefit source? |
| **Identify what was outside project control** | Did external assumptions fail? Did organizational adoption lag? |
| **Recommend remediation** | What actions could close the gap? Who should take them? |
| **Hand to benefit owner** | The benefit owner is accountable for closing the gap post-closure; the project manager hands over the assessment and recommendations |

A project that delivered every output but realized fewer benefits than planned is not necessarily a failed project — but it is a learning opportunity that the organization should not waste. The honest benefits assessment is the first chapter of that learning.

## The Transition to Benefit Owners

At closure, benefit measurement transitions from the project manager to the benefit owner:

| Transition Element | Project Manager's Responsibility | Benefit Owner's Responsibility |
|--------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Measurement method | Documented and validated during the project | Continued post-closure |
| Data sources | Access arranged; data pipeline documented | Data collected and analyzed |
| Baseline and targets | Established in the benefits plan | Updated with actuals at closure |
| Measurement schedule | Defined for post-closure period | Executed; reported to sponsor |
| Leading indicators | Current values provided | Monitored for lagging confirmation |

The handover is a formal event — a meeting, a documented transition, and a commitment from the benefit owner to report on schedule.

## Benefits in Program Closure

Program benefits assessment at closure:

- **Program benefit assessment**: aggregated across all projects — total program benefits realized vs. planned
- **Project benefit assessments**: each project manager submits their benefits assessment; the program manager consolidates
- **Cross-project benefit dependencies**: where a benefit depended on multiple projects, the program manager assesses the integrated result
- **Benefit sustainability**: the program closure assessment includes a sustainability assessment — are benefits likely to persist?

## Practical Applications

### Benefits Assessment Checklist

- [ ] Every planned benefit is assessed: achieved, partially achieved, not achieved, or not yet measurable
- [ ] Variance from target is explained with root cause — within project control or outside it
- [ ] Unplanned benefits are documented — positive outcomes deserve recognition
- [ ] Benefits requiring post-closure measurement are identified with owners and dates
- [ ] The benefits assessment is honest — no inflation to protect the project's reputation
- [ ] Benefit owners formally accept the transition of measurement responsibility
- [ ] The benefits assessment is included in the project closure report

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Is a Problem | Better Approach |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Benefits-by-claim** | Benefits asserted without evidence; the organization plans against fiction | Every claim supported by measurement data |
| **Premature full credit** | Short-term benefits claimed as fully realized when they are only partially realized | Honest status: achieved, partial, not yet measurable |
| **Shortfall buried** | Benefit shortfalls hidden in the report's appendix or omitted entirely | Shortfalls stated plainly with root cause and recommendations |
| **No transition** | The PM measures at closure; nobody measures after; benefits evaporate unobserved | Formal transition to benefit owner with measurement schedule |
| **Variance without explanation** | "Actual: $7; Target: $9" — no explanation of why | Root cause: what drove the variance, what can be done |

## Success Indicators

- Every planned benefit has a measured actual with variance explanation
- The benefits assessment is honest — shortfalls are stated, not hidden
- Benefit owners have accepted measurement responsibility for post-closure benefits
- Unplanned benefits are recognized and documented
- The organization can use the benefits assessment to inform the next project's business case

## Summary

Benefits realization at closure is the project's value verdict: an honest, evidence-based assessment of every planned benefit — achieved, partially achieved, not achieved, or not yet measurable — with variance explained, root causes identified, and recommendations for closing gaps. The project manager who tells the truth about benefits at closure gives the organization the foundation for its next decision; the one who inflates benefits gives the organization a fiction it will plan against. The closure report's benefits section is the project manager's final professional act on the project — and the honesty it contains determines whether the next project's business case is built on evidence or on wishful thinking.
